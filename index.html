AP Statistics FRQ "Magic Phrases" Sentence Frames (Units 1 - 5)

Unit 1: Exploring Data

Describing a Distribution (S.O.C.S.):

"The distribution of [variable in context] is [Skewed Left/Right / Symmetric], centered at a [Median / Mean] of [Value with units], with a spread/IQR/SD of [Value with units], and has [Outliers at Value / No apparent outliers]."

Comparing Two Distributions (MUST use comparative words):

"The median [variable] for [Group A] is greater than [Group B], while both are skewed right. The spread of [Group A] is larger than [Group B], and [Group A] contains an outlier at [Value] whereas [Group B] has no outliers."

Unit 2: Probability & Random Variables

Expected Value E(X) or μ_X (In Context):

"If this [random process] is repeated many, many times in the long run, the average [variable in context] per [trial/item] will be approximately [Value with units]."

Standard Deviation of a Random Variable σ_X (In Context):

"The [variable in context] typically varies by about [σ value with units] from the mean of [μ value]."

Units 3 & 4: Inference (Categorical & Quantitative)

Interpretation of p-value:

"Assuming that [H₀ in context] is true, there is a [p-value] probability of obtaining a sample statistic as extreme as or more extreme than observed, purely by random chance."

Conclusion Stage — Low p-value (p < α):

"Because p-value = [X] < α = [Y], we reject H₀. There IS convincing statistical evidence that [statement of Ha in context]."

Conclusion Stage — High p-value (p ≥ α) [Fail / Do Not Reject]:

"Because p-value = [X] ≥ α = [Y], we fail to reject H₀ (or do not reject H₀). There IS NOT convincing statistical evidence that [statement of Ha in context]." (NEVER write 'accept H₀'!)

Confidence Level Meaning (C% Level):

"If we were to select many random samples of the same size and construct a [C]% confidence interval for each, approximately [C]% of the resulting intervals would capture the true population [parameter in context]."

Unit 5: Linear Regression

Slope of Regression Line (b):

"For each additional 1-[unit] increase in [Explanatory Variable X], the predicted [Response Variable Y] changes by approximately [b value with units]."

Correlation Coefficient (r):

"There is a [strong / moderate / weak], [positive / negative], linear relationship between [Explanatory X] and [Response Y] in context."

Coefficient of Determination (r²):

"Approximately [r² × 100%] of the variation in [Response Variable Y] is accounted for by the linear relationship with [Explanatory Variable X]."
